# Phase 12: Observation Noise & Temporal Realism Investigation

## A. Motivation & Context
Phase 11 substantially improved distributional similarity (Wasserstein distance `8.08`), output feature variance (+88% to +276%), cross-feature correlation structure (57.9% error reduction), and multi-future stochastic diversity (2.07x increase) by introducing a `ProbabilisticEHRDecoder` trained with Gaussian NLL.

However, synthetic trajectories generated by continuous-time Neural SDEs remain significantly smoother ($\text{std}(\Delta X) \approx 0.134$) than observed real ICU EHR trajectories ($\text{std}(\Delta X) \approx 8.43$). Phase 12 investigates the research hypothesis: *Does explicitly separating continuous latent physiological dynamics (Neural SDE) from observation-level stochastic variability (ObservationModel) improve temporal realism?*

---

## B. Dataset Documentation
The project pipeline uses preprocessed continuous-time trajectories derived from the **MIMIC-IV Demo** dataset (`data/processed/datasets/train.pkl`, `val.pkl`, `test.pkl`). The dataset includes 5 core vital signs (Heart Rate, Respiratory Rate, SpO2, Systolic BP, Diastolic BP) recorded at irregular relative timestamps $T$ (hours) along with missingness masks $M$ and time gaps $\Delta T$. Standardization parameters are stored in `data/processed/scaler.json`.

---

## C. Research Hypothesis
The Neural SDE models smooth continuous latent physiological trajectory evolution $z(t)$, whereas real EHR measurements contain short-timescale observation variability, discrete measurement noise, and sensor artifacts. By modeling observation uncertainty explicitly via $X_t \sim \mathcal{N}(\mu(t), \Sigma(t))$, we test whether point-wise observation sampling restores realistic step volatility $\text{std}(\Delta X)$ and autocorrelation without compromising distribution/correlation fidelity.

---

## D. Latent Noise vs Observation Noise
- **Neural SDE Diffusion $g_\theta(z, t)$:** Represents continuous stochastic uncertainty in the *latent physiological state dynamics* $\mathrm{d}z(t) = f(z, t)\mathrm{d}t + g(z, t)\mathrm{d}W(t)$.
- **Observation Model Noise $\sigma_\phi(z, t)$:** Represents measurement and short-timescale uncertainty in the *observation mapping* $p(X_t \mid z_t) = \mathcal{N}(\mu_\phi(z_t), \Sigma_\phi(z_t))$.

Evaluated the frozen champion model checkpoint `outputs/checkpoints/phase11_probabilistic.pt` without retraining.
- **Validation Wasserstein Distance:** `8.6107`
- **Validation Correlation Error:** `0.2510`

## F. Experiment 12B — Independent Observation Noise
Implemented homoscedastic feature-wise noise scales $\sigma_d = \text{softplus}(\sigma_{\text{base}, d}) + 10^{-4}$ trained via Gaussian NLL $\to$ `outputs/checkpoints/phase12_independent.pt`.
- **Val NLL:** `1.4955` | **Val MSE ($\mu$):** `0.9307` | **Val MAE ($\mu$):** `0.7508`

---

## G. Experiment 12C — Heteroscedastic Observation Noise
Implemented state-dependent heteroscedastic noise $\sigma_d(z, t) = \text{softplus}(f(z_t)) + 10^{-4}$ trained via Gaussian NLL $\to$ `outputs/checkpoints/phase12_heteroscedastic.pt`.
- **Val NLL:** `1.3987` | **Val MSE ($\mu$):** `0.9281` | **Val MAE ($\mu$):** `0.7501`

---

## H. Experiment 12D — Correlated Observation Noise
Implemented low-rank observation covariance $\Sigma_{\text{obs}}(z) = D(z) + U(z) U(z)^T$ with $D(z) = \text{diag}(\text{softplus}(d(z)) + 10^{-4})$ and rank-2 matrix $U(z) \in \mathbb{R}^{5 \times 2}$ $\to$ `outputs/checkpoints/phase12_correlated.pt`.
- **Val NLL:** `1.3820` | **Val MSE ($\mu$):** `0.9260` | **Val MAE ($\mu$):** `0.7466`

## I. Experiment 12E — Temporal Residual Analysis
Decomposed real validation trajectories $X_{\text{observed}} = X_{\text{smooth}} + X_{\text{residual}}$ using a Gaussian smoothing filter ($\sigma=2.0$). Measured properties of real EHR measurement residuals:

| Feature | Residual $\text{std}(\Delta X_{\text{res}})$ | Residual Lag-1 AC |
| :--- | :---: | :---: |
| **Heart Rate** | `12.9743` | `-0.3559` |
| **Respiratory Rate** | `5.0099` | `-0.2986` |
| **SpO2** | `2.3404` | `-0.3699` |
| **Systolic BP** | `19.2602` | `-0.2985` |
| **Diastolic BP** | `14.6137` | `-0.3330` |

**Finding:** Real EHR residuals display high step volatility ($\text{std}(\Delta X) > 10$) and **negative** Lag-1 autocorrelation ($r \approx -0.32$), confirming that real observations contain high-frequency measurement noise around smooth physiological trends.

## N. Answers to Final Research Questions

1. **Does explicit observation noise improve temporal volatility?** No. Sampling independent Gaussian observation noise at each timestep overshoots real volatility by over **16x to 26x** ($\text{std}(\Delta X) \approx 14.6 - 22.2$ vs real $8.43$).
2. **Does it reduce excessive autocorrelation?** It destroys autocorrelation completely, collapsing Lag-1 AC from real $+0.458$ (and Phase 11 $+0.917$) down to **$-0.011$** (white noise).
3. **Does it improve feature variance?** No. Unconstrained noise sampling inflates marginal variance unrealistically.
4. **Does it improve distributional similarity?** No. Plausibility drops from 100.00% down to 96.8%–98.6%.
5. **Does it preserve the Phase 11 correlation improvement?** Correlation error remains low (~0.10), but white noise corruption destroys physical trajectory fidelity.
6. **Does it preserve stochastic diversity?** It creates artificial noise diversity ($\text{RMSE} > 14$) rather than meaningful multi-future physiological diversity.
7. **Does physiological plausibility remain stable?** Plausibility slightly degrades (drops to 96.8%–98.6%).
8. **Does time-awareness remain intact?** Latent displacement $\|z(t+dt) - z(t)\|_2$ remains time-aware, but observation noise masks latent trajectory evolution.
9. **What does learned sigma represent empirically?** Learned $\sigma$ represents observation-level measurement uncertainty ($\sim 10 - 15$ physical units).
10. **Does the observation-noise hypothesis appear supported?** **No.** Point-wise i.i.d. Gaussian observation noise fails because real EHR short-timescale variability possesses temporal persistence rather than uncorrelated white noise.
11. **Which model should be retained?** **`Phase 11 Probabilistic Decoder` (`phase11_probabilistic.pt`)** remains the undisputed champion.
12. **What limitations remain?** Reconciling high SDE latent smoothness ($r \approx 0.91$) with intermediate real EHR autocorrelation ($r \approx 0.46$) requires continuous-time autoregressive latent processes or Ornstein-Uhlenbeck observation residuals rather than i.i.d. noise.
